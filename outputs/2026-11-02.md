## Bigger picture: feature pitch

**The pattern:** Two of the three things that keep an older adult on Medicaid or Medicare out of a hospital bed moved through federal channels on the same day, 11/02/26, and neither was matched by a monitored publisher. KFF's brief "Medicaid's Transportation Benefit and the People Who Use It" (11/02/26) reports that non-emergency medical transportation accounts for less than 1% of Medicaid spending and examines how states administer it, what they spend and who rides, explicitly against the current administration's focus on Medicaid fraud. On the same date CMS asked OMB for emergency clearance to revise the information collection behind the Review Choice Demonstration for Home Health Services (CMS-10599/0938-1311), stating that public harm is reasonably likely to occur if normal, non-emergency procedures are used. The ride to care and the visit at home are being rewritten in the same week by the same agency, through paperwork most beneficiaries never see.

**Why it matters:** Both mechanisms serve people who cannot get themselves to a clinic. The transportation benefit is small enough to be invisible in budget terms, under 1% of Medicaid spending, which is exactly what makes it easy to narrow without anyone noticing a line move; the people who lose it are low-income enrollees who use it to reach dialysis and specialists. On the home health side, BLS counted 1,894,200 home health care services jobs in September 2026, up 4.4% year over year, the fastest-growing care setting in this run's data, and the Review Choice Demonstration determines how the claims behind those visits get reviewed. If the ride stops or the visit gets held in review, nothing in a beneficiary's record shows a service was denied; it shows an appointment missed.

**Why pitch this now:** The emergency clearance request is dated 11/02/26 and, by its own terms, is moving faster than the standard Paperwork Reduction Act timetable, which compresses the window in which anyone can read and respond to it. The KFF brief landed the same day. A piece filed this month can describe both while the OMB decision is still pending; next month it describes a decision already made.

**Angle:** Whether the two services that carry care to a low-income older adult who cannot drive, the ride and the home visit, are being quietly narrowed through administrative instruments that carry no public notice to the people who depend on them. Working title: "The Ride and the Visit." For readers who use NEMT or Medicare home health, and for the agencies and area offices that arrange both.
