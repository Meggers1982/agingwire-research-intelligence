## Bigger picture: feature pitch

**The pattern:** The paperwork that stands between an older adult and paid-for care keeps getting harder to argue with, and the federally funded help on the other side of it is being topped up in five-figure increments. An Administration for Community Living notice published 09/10/26 announces a single-source administrative supplement of $226,000 to the Senior Medicare Patrol Resource Center cooperative agreement held by the Northeast Iowa Area Agency on Aging, bringing the FY 2026 total to $1,226,000. On the same day, KFF published two pieces from the other side of the counter: one finding prior authorization is the single biggest burden insured adults report beyond cost, the other breaking down how much of every premium dollar goes to insurer administrative costs and profit and how that share varies across private insurance markets and Medicare. Together they describe a system where the decision to cover something is contested paperwork, and the only named national infrastructure for helping a beneficiary contest it runs on a little over a million dollars a year.

**Why it matters:** The stake is help, not coverage. A beneficiary who suspects an error on a Medicare statement, or who is told a service needs authorization first, is looking for someone to walk it through with them, and the record now names what that support is funded at: $1,226,000 for the national resource center behind the Senior Medicare Patrol network in FY 2026, $226,000 of it added mid-year. The ACL notice does not say how many beneficiaries that reaches, and this piece should not pretend otherwise. What it can say is that prior authorization was named the biggest burden after price in KFF polling, that the share of premium dollars going to overhead and profit is now published and varies by market, and that the counterweight is one cooperative agreement with a dollar figure attached to it.

**Why pitch this now:** The ACL notice is dated 09/10/26 and the award is for FY 2026, so the money and the fiscal year are both live. Both KFF pieces published 09/10/26. And the cost backdrop moved: BLS medical care CPI printed 593.00 in August 2026, up 1.6% year over year, against 593.78 and +1.7% for July 2026, so prices are still climbing but the pace eased between the two prints.

**Angle:** Whether the help an older adult can actually reach when a bill or an authorization looks wrong is sized anything like the paperwork it exists to answer. Working title: "Who answers when the Medicare statement is wrong." For family caregivers and beneficiaries handling somebody else's coverage, with a second read for the agencies-on-aging network that delivers this counseling.
