## Bigger picture: feature pitch

**The pattern:** Medicare runs two separate post-hospital destinations with two separate public scorecards, and this run is the first in which both are current at the same time. CMS refreshed Inpatient Rehabilitation Facility - Provider Data and Long-Term Care Hospital - Provider Data on 09/11/26, the matching national benchmark files (Inpatient Rehabilitation Facility - National Data, Long-Term Care Hospital - National Data) on 08/12/26, and the Inpatient Rehabilitation Facility - Conditions file, first collected in this run, which counts how many times people with Medicare who had certain medical conditions were treated at each facility in the last year. Together those five files let you do the thing a discharge planner does in an afternoon and a family almost never does: put a named facility next to the national number, for your condition, before you agree to go there. The long-term care cluster is the one coherent cluster this run (cohesion 0.322, 10 items); Medicare and Medicaid is again the bigger pile at 26 items across 8 sources and again not one story.

**Why it matters:** The choice between an inpatient rehabilitation facility and a long-term care hospital is usually made in a hospital hallway, by someone else, from a list. All five of these files carry state and county geography, so the comparison is buildable down to the facility a family is actually being offered. What the Conditions file adds is volume: whether the place taking your stroke or hip is one that treated many Medicare patients with that condition last year or a handful. Nothing in these records is new law and nothing here is hidden; the stake is that the comparison exists in public and is not being made on behalf of the people who have to decide in two days.

**Why pitch this now:** The quality files refreshed 09/11/26 and the national files 08/12/26, so facility-level and benchmark numbers are aligned in the same window, which is what a chart needs. Facility Affiliation Data also refreshed 08/18/26, letting the piece show which hospital a rehab facility is tied to. Anything reported off this version stands until the next monthly refresh overwrites it.

**Angle:** Can a family use Medicare's own post-acute files to second-guess a discharge placement, and what do the files show and hide when you try? Working title: "What the Rehab Scorecard Says Before You Sign." For adult children and spouses handling a discharge, and for the post-acute operators whose volume-by-condition numbers are now sitting in public.

## The pitch

Medicare publishes two scorecards for the two places a hospital can send someone who is not ready to go home, and right now both are current: CMS refreshed Inpatient Rehabilitation Facility - Provider Data and Long-Term Care Hospital - Provider Data on 09/11/26, with the matching national benchmark files posted 08/12/26. A fifth file, Inpatient Rehabilitation Facility - Conditions, counts how many times Medicare patients with certain medical conditions were treated at each facility in the last year. I would like to write the piece that shows a family how to use those files in the two days they usually get. I would take one condition, pull facility-level numbers against the national figures, and walk through what the records answer and what they do not. All of it carries state and county geography, so the walkthrough works for a reader anywhere. Roughly 1,200 words with a comparison chart. Next Avenue's readers are the people making this call for a parent.
