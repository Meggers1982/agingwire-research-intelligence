## Bigger picture: feature pitch

**The pattern:** The 2025 reconciliation law's Medicaid provisions now have a state-by-state map, and it landed in the week polling put health costs at the top of the midterm ballot. KFF published "Which States Will Be Affected by Key Medicaid Provisions in the 2025 Reconciliation Law?" on 10/07/26, sorting states by exposure to new work requirements, immigrant eligibility restrictions, and changes to provider taxes and state directed payments. On 10/06/26 KFF's Health Tracking Poll found health care costs tied with gas as voters' top midterm concern, and a companion chart pack put support for Medicare-for-all at two-thirds of the public, a new high that moves sharply once respondents hear arguments on either side. Read together, those three make it possible to say which states are about to change the financing of the program that pays for home care and nursing home care, and to set that against what voters in those states say they are voting on. The wider Medicare and Medicaid cluster, 36 items from 9 sources, shares a tag rather than a subject and should not be worked as one story.

**Why it matters:** Medicaid is the payer behind most long-term services and supports, and the brief says every state is touched through eligibility and financing changes. The financing provisions are the quiet ones: a change to provider taxes or state directed payments reaches a home care program without ever appearing in a beneficiary's mail. KFF's own data note on home- and community-based services waiting lists, 2016 to 2026, says plainly that waiting lists are an incomplete measure of unmet need and are not comparable across states, so the people who lose access first are the hardest to count. For scale, the 2023 American Community Survey puts 5,994,486 residents aged 65 and over in California and 4,630,733 in Florida. What a family gains from this piece is the ability to name the specific provision their own state is applying, rather than reading a national number that describes nobody's program.

**Why pitch this now:** The brief is dated 10/07/26 and the KFF poll says the midterms are about a month away. State decisions on provider taxes and directed payments are being made against that calendar, and the window where a reader can connect a ballot to a benefit closes in early November.

**Angle:** Can an older adult on Medicaid find out, before they vote, which provisions of the 2025 law their own state is about to apply to the program that pays for their care, and which states are quietly changing financing rather than eligibility? Working title: "Fifty Medicaid Programs, One Law." For state health policy readers and for families managing a parent's long-term care coverage.
