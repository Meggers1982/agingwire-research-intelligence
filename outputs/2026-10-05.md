## Bigger picture: feature pitch

**The pattern:** Medicare and Medicaid was the busiest beat in this run, 41 items from 9 sources, and it is not one story: those items share a tag, not a subject. One thread inside it is worth pulling out whole. On 10/05/26 the Federal Trade Commission sent letters to 24 of the nation's largest healthcare services companies reminding them of the legal obligation to give patients timely, accurate and complete pricing information, and that failing to do so can be an unfair or deceptive practice. The same day, RAND published an analysis of Medicare Severity Diagnosis Related Group upcoding in California hospitals between 2018 and 2023, finding upcoding increased payments for commercial stays and raised out-of-pocket costs for some Medicare Advantage patients. One document is about what a patient is told before care. The other is about what the chart says afterward. On 10/02/26 the FTC, with the Utah and Nevada attorneys general, sued a contact lens retailer over advertised prices that were not the prices charged.

**Why it matters:** The RAND finding is the stake, not the letters. How a stay is coded is chosen by nobody the patient can see, is not printed on anything a patient receives, and according to that report moved what some Medicare Advantage enrollees paid out of pocket. Twenty-four companies have now been told in writing what they owe patients on price; no record in this run gives a patient the other half, a way to check the code assigned to their own stay against the care delivered. Medical care prices by the BLS Consumer Price Index stood at 593 (1982-84=100) in August 2026, up 1.6% year over year, which is the backdrop and not the explanation for an individual bill moving.

**Why pitch this now:** The FTC letters and both RAND reports published 10/05/26 and were first collected in this run; the Lens.com complaint is dated 10/02/26. All three carry a coverage_state of gap, meaning monitored publishers work this beat and none matched them. Enforcement framed around price accuracy and a peer analysis of billing codes landing in the same week is a window that closes as soon as one of the health trades writes the letters as a one-day story.

**Angle:** Whether an older patient can establish what their hospital stay was coded as, and what that code did to their share of the bill, at a moment when federal enforcement is aimed at what companies disclose rather than at what they record. Working title: "The Price You Were Quoted and the Code You Were Billed." For Medicare and Medicare Advantage enrollees who have had an inpatient stay in the past year, and the adult children who open the statements.
