## Bigger picture: feature pitch

**The pattern:** HUD reset the price of federal housing credit for fiscal 2027 three days before the fiscal year starts, and the notices doing it arrived together. On 09/28/26 the Federal Register carried an increase in the upfront loan guarantee fee for all new Section 184 Firm Commitments, which HUD frames as keeping the program self-sustaining, and the fee HUD will collect from Section 108 borrowers to offset credit subsidy costs on commitments awarded in Fiscal Year 2027. They sit alongside two notices already in the file: the FY 2027 Fair Market Rents notice of 09/01/26, effective October 1, which sets what a Housing Choice Voucher covers and lists how public housing agencies request a reevaluation under HOTMA, and the Housing Trust Fund FY 2026 allocation notice of 09/04/26, whose eligible grantees are the 50 states, the District of Columbia, Puerto Rico and the territories. Read together, these four documents are the fiscal 2027 arithmetic of subsidized housing: what the government charges to guarantee a loan, and what it will pay toward a rent. None of the fee notices in our capture publishes a dollar figure, so the story is the reset itself, not its size.

**Why it matters:** Older renters and owners at the bottom of the housing market absorb both halves of that arithmetic. Justice in Aging, writing on 09/17/26, put 2025 Census data at 10 million older adults living in poverty. For them the October 1 rent standard is the difference between a voucher that covers an apartment and one that does not, and a higher upfront guarantee fee on a Section 184 commitment is money due at closing rather than spread over a loan. All four notices are gaps or lightly covered against the publishers being monitored, which means the people paying the fee are likelier to learn about it from a closing statement than from a story.

**Why pitch this now:** Fiscal year 2027 begins October 1, which is when the FMRs take effect and the Section 108 fee attaches to new commitments. The Section 184 increase applies to all new Firm Commitments from its effective date forward, so the window in which a borrower could still ask what changed is measured in days. This also follows the HUD-held healthcare loan sale notice of 09/25/26 that we pitched on 09/25/26; that piece was about HUD shedding assets, this one is about HUD repricing the credit it keeps extending.
