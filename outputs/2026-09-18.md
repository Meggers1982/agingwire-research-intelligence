## Bigger picture: feature pitch

**The pattern:** The number of older Americans living in poverty has crossed 10 million while the rate that describes them barely moved, and the states that pay for the other half of their care are writing budgets into a tighter year. Justice in Aging's 09/17/26 post on the Census Bureau's annual poverty report puts the Supplemental Poverty Measure rate at 15.4% among older adults, not a statistically significant change from last year, with the count now over 10 million and up from the prior figure. KFF's 09/18/26 brief reads state revenue and spending data from the National Association of State Budget Officers against the Medicaid budget decisions states face for FY 2027, and its companion 09/18/26 brief on the $50 billion Rural Health Transformation Program sets five years of state grants against the Medicaid spending reductions in the 2025 reconciliation law. That is a reading I am imposing: Medicare and Medicaid is the busiest beat this run at 29 items from eight sources with cohesion 0.04, and financial security at seven items is no tighter, so neither cluster is one story on its own.

**Why it matters:** More than 10 million older adults are below the Supplemental Poverty Measure, and the Center for Retirement Research's 09/02/26 piece on how much Americans rely on Social Security at all income levels says the income side of that picture is thin well above the poverty line. Those same households are the ones state Medicaid budgets cover for long-term services, and nothing in the record lets a family see the two together: the poverty count is federal and national, the budget decision is state and fiscal-year, and the rural grant money is distributed across and within states on a third schedule. The consequence is that a household can be counted in one file and cut in another without either document ever naming the same person.

**Why pitch this now:** The Census figures posted 09/17/26, both KFF briefs carry 09/18/26, and states are setting FY 2027 Medicaid budgets now rather than after the fact. Rural Health Transformation funds are being distributed across and within states during the same window.

**Angle:** Whether the states writing FY 2027 Medicaid budgets are building on top of a poverty count that just passed 10 million older adults, and whether anyone is checking that the rural grant money lands where the reductions do. Working title: "The Floor Under Ten Million." For readers approaching or living on fixed retirement income, and for the state-level providers who serve them.

## The pitch

The Census Bureau's annual poverty report puts the Supplemental Poverty Measure rate for older adults at 15.4%, which is not a statistically significant change from last year, and yet the count behind it has passed 10 million people. I would like to write the piece that explains how a flat rate produces a rising number, and what that means for the states now setting FY 2027 Medicaid budgets. KFF's 09/18/26 brief reads state revenue and spending data from the National Association of State Budget Officers against those decisions, and a companion brief the same day describes the $50 billion Rural Health Transformation Program alongside the Medicaid spending reductions in the 2025 reconciliation law. I would pair both with Census ACS state counts of residents 65 and over, including California at 5,994,486 and Florida at 4,630,733, so readers can size the population their state is budgeting around. About 1,400 words with a state table. Your readers plan around fixed income and state coverage together.
