## Bigger picture: feature pitch

**The pattern:** The October employment print pulls long-term care apart into three labor markets that are no longer moving together. Home health care services reached 1,903.60 thousands of jobs in October 2026, up 4.4% year over year, on average hourly earnings of $28.26 in September, up 2.5%. Continuing care retirement communities and assisted living sat at 1,021.60 thousands of jobs, up 0.5% over twelve months, while paying 5.4% more, at $25.99 an hour. Nursing care facilities landed in between at 1,591.80 thousands of jobs, up 2.1%. Read together, those three BLS series say congregate care is buying raises it cannot convert into headcount, while home-based care is adding people four times faster on slower pay increases.

**Why it matters:** A family choosing assisted living is choosing the setting whose workforce grew half a percent in a year, and whose average wage sits $2.27 an hour below what home health pays, while the two settings recruit from the same pool. For operators, a 5.4% wage increase that produces 0.5% employment growth is money spent holding position. The 11,862 thousands of people aged 65 and over who were working in October are part of the same labor market, not separate from it.

**Why pitch this now:** All six of these series were first collected in this run, the October employment figures are the newest print available, and the September wage figures are the most recent wage data. The home health and nursing care facility series are confirmed gaps against monitored publishers. The CY 2027 rate-setting cycle is in print alongside them, with the Medicare Hospital Outpatient Prospective Payment and Ambulatory Surgical Center final rule published 11/06/26.

**Angle:** Whether congregate senior living can pay its way out of a hiring problem that home-based care is solving with volume, written as "The $2.27 Gap," for senior living and skilled nursing operators and for families weighing a building against care at home.

**Potential headlines:**
- Assisted living is paying 5.4 percent more and barely adding anyone
- Home care hired four times faster than senior living this year
- The aide who would have staffed your parent's building took a home visit job instead

**Potential outlets:**
- **Senior Housing News** — its operator readership is making 2027 labor budgets against exactly the CCRC and assisted living series that moved 0.5% on a 5.4% raise.
- **McKnight's Long-Term Care News** — the nursing care facility line at 1,591.80 thousands of jobs, up 2.1%, is the staffing number its skilled nursing audience is measured by.
- **Skilled Nursing News** — frames the same three series as a competition for workers with business consequences for census and agency spend.
- **Next Avenue** — the consumer version, for readers deciding whether the care they want is better staffed at home than in a building.

## Story ideas

**Senior living added almost no staff this year**
*BLS: Continuing care retirement communities and assisted living employment, October 2026 — 1,021.60 thousands of jobs (+0.5% year over year)*  
- Angle: A straight read of the October CCRC and assisted living employment line, 1,021.60 thousands of jobs and up 0.5% in twelve months, for operators and for families touring buildings.
- Hook: Half a percent of growth in a year, against 4.4% in home health care services, in the setting families are most often sold on staffing.
- For readers: When you tour a community, ask how many caregiving and dining positions are open today and how that compares with a year ago, and ask what share of shifts are filled by agency staff.
- For the trade: If headcount is flat while wages rise, the constraint on move-ins is labor supply rather than demand, which changes what an occupancy forecast is worth.
- Why it matters: A building that cannot grow its staff cannot absorb a resident whose needs grow, and you find that out after you move in unless you ask first.
- Also: National series only, but charts cleanly against the home health and nursing facility lines; coverage is light rather than absent.
- Pitch to: McKnight's Senior Living, Senior Housing News

**The raise is real and it still trails home care pay**
*BLS: Assisted living and CCRC average hourly earnings, September 2026 — 25.99 dollars per hour (+5.4% year over year)*
