## Bigger picture: feature pitch

**The pattern:** The long-term care cluster is the one place this run where the items genuinely share a subject: the full CMS nursing home file suite refreshed 08/01/26 (Provider Information, Health Deficiencies, Fire Safety Deficiencies, Penalties, Ownership, Survey Summary, MDS Quality Measures, Nursing Home Data Collection Intervals), the BLS August 2026 employment series for nursing care facilities (1,590.20 thousand jobs, +2.1% year over year), home health care services (1,896.40 thousand, +5.0%) and continuing care retirement communities and assisted living (1,026.90 thousand, +1.4%), and Justice in Aging's Medicaid Home and Community-Based Services primer (08/20/26). Put side by side, they say something specific: every facility-level accountability file in this run describes nursing homes, while hiring is growing more than twice as fast in home health as in the buildings those files grade. The wage series point the same direction, with home health average hourly earnings at $28.04 in July 2026 and assisted living and CCRC earnings at $25.65. Separately, Medicare and Medicaid was the busiest beat of the run at 26 items from seven sources, but those items share a tag and nothing else, from a CMS medical equipment supplier file refreshed 08/30/26 to a KFF data note on Medicaid coverage for women dated 08/26/26. That is not one story and should be worked as separate leads.

**Why pitch this now:** The money underneath these settings is being rewritten. A KFF analysis dated 08/14/26 estimates $60 billion in federal Medicaid spending across 37 states including the District of Columbia would likely exceed the new federal limits on state directed payments for hospital services under the 2025 reconciliation law, with California, Illinois, Kentucky, Texas, North Carolina, Louisiana and Arizona among the states facing the biggest potential reductions, and a companion KFF brief on 08/20/26 lays out how provider tax rules are changing. Medical care CPI rose 1.7% year over year in July 2026. Payment pressure and a shifting labor base land on the same operators at the same time.

**Angle:** "Where the care jobs went," a national data story pairing the CMS nursing home files with the three BLS care-setting employment and wage series, written for families choosing between a facility and care at home, with an operator sidebar on recruiting competition across the three settings.

**Potential headlines:**

- Three care settings, three very different hiring curves

## Story ideas

**American Community Survey 2023 state aging and housing profile (52 states)**
- Hook: State-level counts for 65+, 65-74, 75-84 and 85+ populations sit alongside median household income and housing tenure in one 2023 ACS 5-year file, with California at 5,994,486 residents aged 65 and older and Florida at 4,630,733.
- For readers: If you are weighing a move, look up your current state and your shortlist state in the same table and compare three things before you commit: the size of the 85+ population, the split between owners and renters, and median household income. A state with a big 65+ headline number is not the same as a state with a big 85+ population, and it is the 85+ number that predicts what care and housing you will be competing for.
- For the trade: The 85+ column crossed with housing tenure is a site-selection screen: it separates markets where prospects are equity-rich homeowners from markets where they will arrive as renters with no sale proceeds, which changes entry-fee and rental product mix state by state.
- Also: Fifty-two geographies, no monitored publisher matched it, and it charts cleanly as a ranking or a two-axis scatter. Strong fit for Retirement Living and Multi-Housing News - Senior Housing.

**Fair Market Rents for the Housing Choice Voucher Program, Moderate Rehabilitation Single Room Occupancy Program, and Other Programs, Fiscal Year 2027**
- Hook: HUD published the FY 2027 Fair Market Rents on 09/01/26, along with the methods behind them and the procedure for public housing agencies to request reevaluations under the Housing Opportunity Through Modernization Act of 2016.
- For readers: If you rent with a Housing Choice Voucher, the numbers that set your subsidy are adjusted to take effect October 1. Find your metro's figure, compare it with what your landlord is asking, and ask your public housing agency directly whether it intends to request a reevaluation for your area.
- For the trade: For affordable senior housing owners and the agencies they work with, the reevaluation request window is the actionable part of this notice, and voucher-heavy portfolios should be modeling FY 2027 rents against operating cost growth now rather than in October.
- Also: Metro-level, so it localizes almost anywhere, and year-over-year FMR change by metro is a straightforward chart. No monitored publisher matched it, so this is a real opening.

**Housing Trust Fund: Fiscal Year 2026 Allocation Notice**
- Hook: HUD's FY 2026 Housing Trust Fund allocation notice published 09/04/26, the newest item in the run, covering the program established by the Housing and Economic Recovery Act of 2008 with eligible grantees being the 50 states, the District of Columbia, Puerto Rico, American Samoa, Guam and the Commonwealth of the Northern Mariana Islands.
- For readers: These dollars do not come to you directly; they pass through your state's housing agency. If you or a parent are on a waiting list for affordable housing, ask the state agency how it plans to use its Housing Trust Fund allocation this year and which projects are in line for it.
- For the trade: Nonprofit developers can read state allocations as a pipeline signal, and the notice is the moment to line up applications against the state's stated priorities rather than after awards are announced.
- Also: Published the day of the run and unmatched by monitored publishers. State-by-state allocation makes a map.

**BLS: Home health care services employment, August 2026 — 1,896.40 thousands of jobs (+5.0% year over year)**
- Hook: Home health care services employment reached 1,896.40 thousand jobs in August 2026, up 5.0% year over year, the fastest growth of any care setting in this run.
- For readers: More hiring does not automatically mean more reliable coverage in your house. When you interview an agency, ask how many aides it has added in the past year, how often shifts go unfilled, and who shows up when your regular aide is out.
- For the trade: Home health is pulling workers at a rate nursing homes and assisted living are not matching, which makes it the wage-setter in most local labor markets. Operators in the other two settings should be pricing their offers against home health, not against each other.
- Also: Monthly series with a long history, so a three-line chart of the three care settings writes itself. Unmatched by monitored publishers.

**BLS: Nursing care facilities employment, August 2026 — 1,590.20 thousands of jobs (+2.1% year over year)**
- Hook: Nursing care facilities employed 1,590.20 thousand people in August 2026, up 2.1% year over year, growth less than half the home health rate.
- For readers: Staffing figures for individual nursing homes are in the CMS Provider Information file that feeds the Five-Star Rating System. Look up the facility you are considering, then ask the administrator directly whether staffing has increased in the past year and how many shifts run short.
- For the trade: National growth of 2.1% is the benchmark to hold facility-level hiring against, and pairing it with the CMS staffing measures and Nursing Home Data Collection Intervals file shows whether a market's recovery is broad or concentrated in a few chains.
- Also: Unmatched by monitored publishers, and it pairs directly with the CMS files refreshed 08/01/26 for a staffing-versus-quality treatment.

**BLS: Assisted living and CCRC average hourly earnings, July 2026 — 25.65 dollars per hour (+3.2% year over year)**
- Hook: Average hourly earnings in assisted living and continuing care retirement communities reached $25.65 in July 2026, up 3.2% year over year, while home health care services averaged $28.04, up 2.0%.
- For readers: Labor is the largest cost in any community you tour. When you get the fee schedule, ask what last year's increase was, how much of it went to caregiver pay, and what the community's turnover rate is now compared with a year ago.
- For the trade: Assisted living wages are rising faster in percentage terms but remain more than two dollars an hour below home health, which is the retention arithmetic in a sentence. Compensation reviews built on the internal budget alone will miss the competing offer.
- Also: Two BLS series that plot against each other on one chart. Both unmatched by monitored publishers.

**BLS: Employment level, 65 years and over, August 2026 — 11,753 thousands of people (+2.1% year over year)**
- Hook: Some 11,753 thousand people aged 65 and over were employed in August 2026, up 2.1% from a year earlier.
- For readers: If you are still working past 65, ask your employer's benefits office in writing how its plan coordinates with Medicare and what your enrollment obligations are while you remain covered at work. Do not assume one arrangement replaces the other automatically.
- For the trade: This is also a labor supply story for the aging services sector, which recruits from the same cohort it serves. Operators building part-time and flexible schedules have a pool of 11.75 million people to recruit from.
- Also: Long-running monthly series, easy trend line, unmatched by monitored publishers. Good fit for the consumer finance titles.

**BLS: Consumer Price Index, medical care, July 2026 — 593.78 index 1982-84=100 (+1.7% year over year)**
- Hook: Medical care prices rose 1.7% year over year in July 2026 by the Consumer Price Index measure.
- For readers: Use 1.7% as your yardstick. If a plan, provider or community raises what you pay by substantially more than that this year, ask what specifically is driving the increase and what the same service cost last year.
- For the trade: A 1.7% medical care CPI is a hard external reference in rate conversations and budget defenses, and it is a useful contrast with the 3.2% wage growth in assisted living, where input costs are outrunning the general medical price measure.
- Also: Unmatched by monitored publishers. Charts well against the wage series for a cost-squeeze graphic.

**Analysis: At Least 37 States Have Medicaid State Directed Payments for Hospital Services That Could Be Reduced by the 2025 Reconciliation Law Limits**
- Hook: KFF estimates that roughly $60 billion in federal Medicaid spending across 37 states including the District of Columbia would likely exceed the new federal limits on state directed payments for hospital services once fully implemented, with California, Illinois, Kentucky, Texas, North Carolina, Louisiana and Arizona among those facing the biggest potential reductions.
- For readers: If you live in one of the named states and depend on a single nearby hospital, this is the year to ask which service lines it expects to keep. Find out now where the next-closest emergency department and inpatient beds are, before you need them in a hurry.
- For the trade: Hospital payment exposure travels downstream to skilled nursing and post-acute referral relationships. Operators in the seven named states should be modeling what reduced hospital payments do to discharge patterns and to partners' willingness to hold beds.
- Also: Named states make this immediately localizable, the $60 billion figure carries a headline, and no monitored publisher matched it.

**Medicare Will Spend More Than $13 Billion on the Medicare Advantage Quality Bonus Program in 2026**
- Hook: A KFF issue brief puts Medicare Advantage quality bonus spending above $13 billion in 2026.
- For readers: Before you renew a Medicare Advantage plan, look up its star rating rather than the marketing. The rating is what determines whether the plan draws from this bonus pool, and it is the single comparable number across plans in your county.
- For the trade: For providers and senior living operators with Medicare Advantage contracts, bonus economics shape what plans can afford at the negotiating table, and $13 billion is the size of the pool those conversations sit inside.
- Also: National figure, strong standalone stat, unmatched by monitored publishers. Fits Fierce Healthcare or the consumer Medicare titles equally.

**CMS refreshed dataset: Hospice care - National and State CAHPS Hospice Survey Data**
- Hook: CMS refreshed both the national and the state average CAHPS Hospice Survey scores for Medicare-certified hospice providers on 08/07/26, giving families two comparison points for the same measures.
- For readers: When a hospital or physician hands you a list of hospices, ask each one how it scored on the CAHPS Hospice Survey and compare that with your state's average. Family members who used the hospice answered those questions, and the state figure tells you whether a provider is ahead of or behind its neighbors.
- For the trade: Having national and state means refreshed together lets hospice operators benchmark precisely rather than against a single national number, and it gives quality teams a defensible target for the measures where their state runs above the national average.
- Also: This is a refreshed public data file rather than a news event, so treat it as the spine of an analysis. Palliative and hospice care was the tightest cluster in the run at 0.457 cohesion, and state means chart on a map.

**CMS refreshed dataset: Penalties**
- Hook: CMS refreshed its file of fines and payment denials received by nursing homes in the last three years on 08/01/26, alongside the Health Deficiencies, Ownership and Survey Summary files.
- For readers: Before you sign an admission agreement, look up the facility in the penalties file and the health deficiencies file, then check the ownership file to see who actually owns it. Bring the specific citations to your tour and ask what was corrected, when, and by whom.
- For the trade: Joining penalties to the ownership file surfaces chain-level patterns rather than one-off facility problems, which is the version of this data that matters for acquisition due diligence and for insurers pricing a portfolio.
- Also: A refreshed reference file, presented one citation per row, with state and county geography, so it supports a county-level map or a chain ranking. Pairs naturally with the BLS nursing care facilities employment series.
