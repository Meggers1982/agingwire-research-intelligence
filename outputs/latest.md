- **The pattern:** On 08/01/26 CMS refreshed almost the entire public nursing home record in a single stroke: Provider Information, Health Deficiencies, Fire Safety Deficiencies, Penalties, Ownership, Survey Summary, MDS Quality Measures, Inspection Dates, Citation Code Look-up, Nursing Home Data Collection Intervals, Medicare Claims Quality Measures and the Skilled Nursing Facility Quality Reporting Program file, all of it broken to states and counties. In the same window BLS put nursing care facilities employment at 1,590.20 thousand jobs for August 2026, up 2.1% year over year, against home health care services at 1,896.40 thousand, up 5.0%. The policy writing that landed alongside points away from the building: Justice in Aging's Medicaid home and community-based services primer on 08/20/26 and the Center for Retirement Research's comparison of long-term care in Israel and the U.S. on 09/03/26. These four sources cohere on one thread, and the thread is a mismatch. The most searchable, most granular public accountability data in American long-term care describes the setting whose workforce is growing slowest, while the setting adding jobs at more than twice that rate has no equivalent citation, penalty or ownership file in this run. Seventeen of the 18 items in the cluster are unmatched by monitored publishers, and the newest is one day old.
- **Why pitch this now:** HUD's FY 2027 Fair Market Rents notice published 09/01/26 and takes effect October 1, and the Housing Trust Fund FY 2026 allocation notice followed on 09/04/26, so the affordability side of aging in place is being repriced this month. On the payment side, KFF's 08/20/26 brief lays out how provider tax rules are changing under the 2025 reconciliation law and its implementing regulations, which reaches the state Medicaid financing behind both nursing facility care and HCBS. Families weighing facility care against care at home are making that call in a month when the money rules on both sides moved.
- **Angle:** "The Public Record Ends at the Front Door," a consumer-first investigation for adult children comparing a nursing home against home-based care, built on the refreshed CMS files and the BLS employment and wage series, with a trade companion on what operators can and cannot be benchmarked against.
- **Potential headlines:** "Every Nursing Home Fine and Citation Just Refreshed and Nobody Told Families"; "Home Health Is Adding Workers Twice as Fast as Nursing Homes"; "The Care Setting With the Best Public Data Is Not the One Growing"
- **Potential outlets:** Seniors Guide, Tier 1 senior living consumer, which can turn the CMS files into a look-up-your-home walkthrough; SeniorLiving.org, Tier 2 consumer resource, where the comparison stands up as evergreen decision guidance; McKnight's Long-Term Care News, Tier 1 long-term care trade, for the deficiency, penalty and ownership file angle; Home Health Care News, Tier 1 home care and home health trade, for the 5.0% employment growth against the thin public quality record.
