## Bigger picture: feature pitch

**The pattern:** On 08/01/26 CMS refreshed nearly the entire public accountability record for nursing homes in a single pass — Provider Information (the file behind the Five-Star Rating System), Health Deficiencies, Fire Safety Deficiencies, Penalties, Ownership, Survey Summary, Inspection Dates, Citation Code Look-up, Nursing Home Data Collection Intervals, MDS Quality Measures, Medicare Claims Quality Measures and the Skilled Nursing Facility Quality Reporting Program provider file. BLS put nursing care facilities employment at 1,590.20 thousand jobs in August 2026, up 2.1% year over year, against home health care services at 1,896.40 thousand, up 5.0%. Justice in Aging's 08/20/26 primer on Medicaid home and community-based services lays out the policy gaps that limit access to the community alternative, and the Center for Retirement Research's 09/03/26 comparison of long-term care in Israel and the United States puts the same structural problem in relief. The substantive thread is an accountability asymmetry: the institutional side of long-term care is the most exhaustively documented and the slowest-growing, while the home-based side families are steered toward is growing more than twice as fast with far less published about who delivers it and how well.

**Why pitch this now:** KFF's 08/20/26 brief on Medicaid and provider taxes describes rules changing under the 2025 reconciliation law and the regulations implementing it, and summarizes which changes may affect each state. Medicaid is the payer standing behind much of this care, and the financing under it is moving this fiscal year. The CMS files are current as of 08/01/26 and carry state and county geography, so a state-level read is available before those changes land.

**Angle:** "The Paper Trail Your Nursing Home Leaves" — a families-choosing-a-facility explainer built on the refreshed CMS files, with a companion trade read on why the documented side of the sector is the one adding the fewest workers.
