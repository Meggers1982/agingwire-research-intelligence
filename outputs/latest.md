## Bigger picture: feature pitch

**The pattern:** CMS refreshed the full set of files that describe a dialysis facility inside one month, and read together they support a comparison that no single file makes: "Dialysis Facility - Listing by Facility" (10/22/26) gives every Medicare-registered clinic with address, phone and services; "Dialysis Facility - State Averages" and "Dialysis Facility - National Averages" (both 10/22/26) give the benchmarks for common quality measures; and the In-Center Hemodialysis CAHPS survey files at facility, state and national level (all 10/06/26) give what patients themselves said about the facility, its doctors and staff. One clinic can now be set against its own state and against the country on both what is measured about it and what the people treated there reported. The Social Security and medical-inflation thread ran on 10/26/26 and is not this; dialysis has not been worked at all in recent runs.

**Why it matters:** For an older adult starting in-center hemodialysis, the standardized patient evaluation of the facility, the facility's doctors and the staff exists in exactly one public place, and it is this survey. A family that does not open the file chooses on proximity and a referral. The scale is honest and limited: these are reference files, not a finding, and CMS publishes no count of affected patients in them. What changes is what a family can look up — a named clinic's result, the state average next to it, and the national average next to that, in files carrying the same refresh month.

**Why pitch this now:** The dialysis quality and roster files carry a 10/22/26 date and the patient-survey files 10/06/26, so the comparison is current for the rest of the year. The timing pressure comes from coverage churn: KFF reported on 10/27/26 that 4.3 million Medicare beneficiaries — 3.1 million Medicare Advantage drug plan enrollees and 1.2 million stand-alone drug plan enrollees — will have their plan terminated at the end of 2026, with another 1.4 million in plans consolidating. Beneficiaries re-examining coverage this fall are also re-examining where they get treated.

**Angle:** Whether a dialysis patient can tell a good clinic from a close one, now that federal files carry the facility roster, the clinical state and national averages and the patients' own ratings on the same refresh cycle. Working title: "What the Patients at Your Dialysis Clinic Actually Said." For older adults, adult children managing a parent's kidney care, and the operators who will be asked about their scores.
