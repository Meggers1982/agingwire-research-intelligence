## Bigger picture: feature pitch

**The pattern:** The price of doing nothing during Medicare open enrollment just got a number. KFF's analysis of 10/30/26 projects the average monthly premium for stand-alone Part D prescription drug plans at $51 in 2027, up 50 percent from $34 in 2026, for enrollees who do not switch into lower-premium plans before open enrollment closes December 7; the companion KFF brief "Medicare Part D Premiums and Cost Sharing in 2027," also 10/30/26, sets that premium against what enrollees pay at the counter. Running underneath it, the Federal Register proposed rule of 10/20/26 would implement sections 6219, 6220 and 6224 of the Consolidated Appropriations Act, 2026, adjusting Part D cost-sharing reductions for low-income individuals, requiring accurate provider lists and addressing pharmacy benefit manager accountability. The default price is being set in the same weeks the rules governing the enrollees least able to absorb it are being rewritten. Medicare and Medicaid was the busiest beat this run, 32 items from eight sources, but those items share a tag rather than a subject; this Part D thread is the lead, not the cluster.

**Why it matters:** An enrollee who keeps the plan they have pays, on the projection, $17 more a month, roughly $204 across 2027, and KFF is explicit that the increase is conditional on not shopping. Set that against the 3.6 percent Social Security cost-of-living increase effective December 2026 in the Federal Register notice of 10/26/26 and a meaningful share of next year's raise is spoken for by one line item, for households who never see a bill explaining why. The low-income cost-sharing adjustments in the 10/20/26 proposed rule decide how much of that lands on people already receiving help.

**Why pitch this now:** Open enrollment runs through December 7, which gives the piece a hard expiry and makes it useless in January. Both KFF analyses carry 10/30/26 dates, and the CY 2027 rulemaking they sit beside is landing in the same window, including the End-Stage Renal Disease Prospective Payment System final rule published 10/30/26.

**Angle:** Whether Part D's design assumption, that beneficiaries shop every year, still protects anyone when the penalty for staying put is a 50 percent premium increase and the cost-sharing rules for low-income enrollees are simultaneously in flux. Working title: "The Price of Staying Put." For older adults with stand-alone drug coverage and the adult children who do the comparing.
