## Bigger picture: feature pitch

**The pattern:** Seven CMS hospice files refreshed between 08/07/26 and 08/19/26, and read together they support something the separate downloads never did — a family can start from a zip code and end at a named, scored provider. Hospice - Zip Data (08/18/26) lists the zip codes each hospice serves. Hospice - General Information (08/19/26) carries address, phone and ownership. Hospice care - Provider CAHPS Hospice Survey Data (08/07/26) gives each provider's scores on the Consumer Assessment of Healthcare Providers and Systems Hospice Survey measures, and the State and National CAHPS files, both refreshed 08/07/26, supply the averages those scores can be judged against. Hospice - Provider Data and Hospice - State Data (08/07/26) add quality of patient care measures at both levels. This is the one cluster in the run that genuinely hangs together, and it has not been worked as a story.

**Why it matters:** A hospice is usually chosen in days, on a name supplied by a discharge planner, and until these files are joined a family has no way to know whether that name is one of several serving their address or the only one, whether its survey scores sit above or below the state mean, or who owns it. The published record now answers all three questions for Medicare-certified hospice providers nationwide, at provider, state and national level. What is at stake is not the file moving; it is that the comparison exists and almost nobody performs it at the moment the decision is made.

**Why pitch this now:** The whole set refreshed inside a thirteen-day window, 08/07/26 to 08/19/26, so provider scores, state means and national means share a vintage and can be joined cleanly. At the next monthly refresh those vintages drift apart again. The nursing home and Medicare-technology threads have carried the last four pitches (09/03/26, 09/04/26, 09/05/26, 09/06/26); the hospice record has sat underneath them untouched.

**Angle:** Whether the hospice a family is handed is the one they would have chosen had they seen the service-area map, the ownership line and the survey scores side by side. Working title: "The Hospice That Serves Your Zip Code." For families arranging end-of-life care on short notice, and for hospice operators whose provider-level scores now sit next to a published state mean.

## The pitch

Seven CMS hospice files refreshed between 08/07/26 and 08/19/26, and read together they support a lookup your readers cannot get anywhere else: enter a zip code, get the hospices whose service area covers it, then check each one's scores on the CAHPS Hospice Survey measures against its state average and the national mean. Hospice - Zip Data carries the service areas, Hospice - General Information carries address, phone and ownership, and the provider, state and national CAHPS files carry the scores, all refreshed within thirteen days of each other so the vintages match. I would write this at about 1,200 words with a state-level table showing where hospice providers sit relative to the national means, plus a short list of what to ask a hospice whose scores fall below its state average. SeniorLiving.org already publishes state comparisons for readers choosing care, and hospice is the choice your audience makes in days rather than months. Everything rests on the published CMS files.
