## Bigger picture: feature pitch

**The pattern:** Medicaid coverage now survives or fails at two paperwork steps rather than one, and both records landed inside a month. KFF's Tracking Implementation of the 2025 Reconciliation Law: Medicaid Work Requirements Data and Policies published 09/09/26 with state and national data on what each state is asking enrollees to document, and no monitored publisher has matched it. KFF's prior authorization analysis, dated Aug 13, 2026, found insurers denied at least 1 in 8 standard prior authorization requests in 2025 across Medicare Advantage, Medicaid managed care and the federally facilitated ACA Marketplaces, with denial rates varying widely by insurer. One record covers getting in, the other covers getting care once you are in. The wider Medicare and Medicaid tag was the busiest beat this run, 26 items from 7 sources at 0.05 cohesion, and it is not one story; these two items are the thread inside it worth reporting, and Justice in Aging's Medicaid Home and Community-Based Services for Older Adults primer (08/20/26) is the third leg if the piece needs a long-term care spine.

**Why it matters:** For an older Medicaid enrollee or the family caregiver filing on their behalf, eligibility on paper is now two administrative tests away from an actual service. The prior authorization figure is the one hard number: at least one standard request in eight was denied in 2025, in the same three markets that carry Medicare Advantage enrollees and Medicaid managed care members. The work requirements tracker says what a state expects to be proved and how, which is what determines whether someone who qualifies stays enrolled. The KFF analysis itself notes the data's limitations, so the second finding of the story is what still cannot be looked up: denial reasons and outcomes by insurer, and documentation burden by state, are not published at a level a family can use.

**Why pitch this now:** The work requirements tracker went up 09/09/26 and is being updated as states stand the requirements up, so the state-by-state design table is current this month and will not be next. The prior authorization data covers 2025, the first full year against which the reconciliation law's coverage changes can be read.

**Angle:** Whether coverage that survives the 2025 reconciliation law on paper survives the documentation and authorization steps that come after it, and what the published data still will not tell an enrollee. Working title: "Covered on Paper." For payer and provider policy readers, and for the caregivers who file the forms.

## The pitch

A Medicaid enrollee can be eligible and still lose care twice over: once at the documentation step, once at the authorization step. KFF published its tracker of Medicaid work requirements under the 2025 Reconciliation Law on 09/09/26, with state and national data on what each state is requiring as it stands the rules up. Set against KFF's prior authorization analysis of 2025 data, which found insurers denied at least 1 in 8 standard requests across Medicare Advantage, Medicaid managed care and the federally facilitated ACA Marketplaces with denial rates varying widely by insurer, the two records describe the same machinery from either end. I would write 1,200 to 1,500 words: what a state's chosen design asks an older enrollee or a family caregiver to prove, what the denial rates say about coverage after enrollment, and where the published data stops short of an answer. Your readers sit on both sides of that paperwork, payer and provider, which is why I am bringing it here first.

*Prior Authorization Metrics Provide New Insights into Insurer Practices, but Gaps Remain*
