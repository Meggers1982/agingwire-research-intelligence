Read together, these three make it possible to answer a question an announced percentage cannot: what the December increase is worth to a beneficiary whose drug costs and benefit eligibility are tested against income. These are separate documents from separate bodies, not one story arriving three ways.

**Why it matters:** The 3.6 percent is the same for every beneficiary. What it does to the rest of a household's year is not. The notice itself says the increase and the wage index drive other program parameters, and the 10/20/26 proposed rule would change how Part D cost-sharing reductions are calculated for low-income individuals, which is the same population for whom a few dollars of additional monthly income is consequential. The estate recovery analysis names the other end of the same ledger: what a state can take back after death from an older adult who was poor enough to qualify for Medicaid. A reader who only sees the percentage cannot tell whether their own net position improves in December.

**Why pitch this now:** The increase is effective December 2026 and the determining notice published 10/26/26. The Part D proposed rule published 10/20/26 is still at the proposed stage, which means comment is open and the final numbers are not fixed. A December-effective number explained in November is useful; explained in January it is history.
